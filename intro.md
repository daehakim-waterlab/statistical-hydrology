# Statistical Hydrology

An online textbook for the undergraduate Statistical Hydrology course in the Department of Civil Engineering, Jeonbuk National University, by [Daeha Kim](https://hydroclimatol.com/).

All fifteen chapters and both appendices are now available. The Colab labs are being prepared and will be added as they are completed.

Rainfall, streamflow and groundwater levels vary in ways that cannot be predicted in detail, yet structures must be designed and water must be allocated for an uncertain future. This book develops the probability and statistics needed to describe that variability, to estimate design values from short records, to test whether records have changed, and to model how hydrological variables depend on one another and on their own past.

Each chapter starts from a general statistical idea, works it out with a hydrological example, and shows how to compute it in Python. Readers are expected to know basic calculus and the hydrology of the companion book [Engineering Hydrology](https://hydroclimatol.com/engineering-hydrology/), whose design-rainfall and design-flood chapters use the frequency analysis developed here. The graduate book [Physical Hydrology](https://hydroclimatol.com/physical-hydrology/) treats the processes behind the numbers, and [*A Brief History of Fluid Mechanics and Water Sciences*](https://hydroclimatol.com/water-history/) tells how statistical hydrology began.

Figures and tables are generated by the Python code that accompanies them, which runs every time the book is built. Worked examples use invented numbers or synthetic data. Several chapters end with a lab, a Colab notebook that applies the methods to real, openly available records.

The whole book is also available as a [PDF](https://hydroclimatol.com/statistical-hydrology/statistical-hydrology.pdf).
